## What Makes Corrugated Mailer Boxes Useful?

Corrugated packaging is constructed using layers of paperboard, including a fluted inner layer that provides structural support between outer liner sheets. The construction can be selected according to the product and the conditions the package is expected to encounter.

For businesses, this means the packaging can be evaluated based on more than appearance.

### Protection During Transportation

A product can experience movement, stacking, compression, and repeated handling during its journey to the customer. Selecting an appropriate corrugated construction can help create packaging that is better suited to those conditions.

The right design also considers the product itself. A lightweight retail item may not require the same box construction as a heavier industrial component.

## Single-Wall, Double-Wall, and Triple-Wall Options

The construction of the corrugated board can influence how the finished box performs.

### Single-Wall Construction

Single-wall corrugated packaging can be suitable for many everyday shipping and fulfillment applications. It offers a practical balance between structural support, weight, and material usage.

### Double-Wall Construction

When products are heavier or require additional support, double-wall corrugated construction can provide greater structural strength. It can be useful for products that experience more demanding shipping or storage conditions.

### Triple-Wall Construction

Triple-wall construction provides an additional level of board structure for demanding applications. It may be considered when packaging heavier products or applications where increased durability is important.

The best option depends on the product rather than simply choosing the thickest available material.

## How to Choose the Right Corrugated Mailer Box

Selecting packaging should begin with the product rather than the box.

### 1. Measure the Product

Record the actual length, width, and height of the item. If protective material will be placed around it, account for that space as well.

### 2. Determine the Product Weight

Weight helps determine the level of board strength and structural support that may be appropriate.

### 3. Understand the Shipping Environment

Consider whether the package will be shipped locally, transported long distances, stacked with other cartons, or handled through multiple distribution points.

### 4. Consider Internal Protection

Some products can be shipped directly inside the mailer, while fragile or sensitive products may require inserts, partitions, foam, or other cushioning.

### 5. Decide Whether Printing Is Important

If customers receive the package directly, printed branding may add value to the overall experience.

### 6. Think About Your Order Volume

Packaging needs can change as your business grows. A packaging solution that works for a small product launch should ideally be scalable when order volumes increase.
